## Foreword

People always need some dreams of their own to pursue. Studying for the sake of GPA easily feels dull; studying for a dream, however hard, is a joy.

I do have dreams. Yet lately I've been feeling lost, because AI is advancing so fast. In the age of AI, what can we do?

The first thing I want to answer is: what is AI good at, and what does it struggle to do well?

In fields where there is a correct answer and feedback comes immediately, AI learns and evolves very quickly. But many things are not like arithmetic or multiple-choice questions, where a correct answer is available right away.

For example, in the iteration and development of high-performance networking, you have to consider not just whether performance improved a little today, but that today's technical decisions may become the infrastructure of tomorrow—and what their long-term impact will be. That kind of thing is very hard to train for.

Or take AI doing mathematics: it may solve problems quickly, but that speed often comes from brute-force search rather than mathematical intuition. It struggles to distill elegant theorems, to pose problems worth solving, and to develop meaningful new tools in the course of solving problems—the very things that keep mathematics alive as a discipline—because there's no fast feedback signal from which AI could learn them.

When writing documentation, AI may have a hard time knowing what should be emphasized and what should be skipped briefly, or whether the logical order and references make it easy for readers to follow, so it often just piles content together—after all, such judgments and trade-offs rarely come with a clear signal. In literary writing, AI can produce beautiful prose, yet it cannot replace the diary a unique individual writes for themselves—because AI cannot express, in fitting language, how that person actually felt at the time, and at this level, short-term signals of correctness are missing.

So wherever verification is hard and feedback is slow, AI is relatively weak.

Human value lies in being able to make judgments and trade-offs based on our own thinking precisely where AI can't get fast feedback and can't iterate. This requires mastering a lot of foundational knowledge, and gaining hands-on experience even in things AI can do; with that practical experience, we can make judgments and trade-offs at a higher level. At the same time, learning good AI skills and using AI more efficiently than others is also very important.

So, what can I do?

First, build up understanding—which is one of the reasons I started this blog.

No matter how the world develops, I believe one thing stays the same: human understanding always matters.

I hope to accumulate foundational knowledge so that in the future I can make judgments where AI struggles to make trade-offs; to build an understanding of the frontiers of specific fields; and to learn where the boundaries of AI's abilities lie—what it can and cannot do. From a learning perspective, organizing a blog is a process of internalizing experience: only by organizing things by hand and actively producing output can I improve my skills and understanding. It also helps me look back and reflect, since repeated comparison is often what leads to better absorption and internalization.

Second, make good use of AI.

People use AI at very different levels, and their productivity and efficiency differ accordingly. I'll also share AI usage tips on this blog as I go.

And then there are connections between people.

In this society, people always need help from others, or help others to create value for society. We need to learn to proactively reach out by email to broaden our horizons. I hope this blog can help people get to know each other and help one another, and I also hope to find some opportunities by showing who I am.

I'm a bit clearer now about what I should do, but which research direction exactly? Should I continue with HPC-AI, work on high-performance networking, do AI safety, embodied intelligence, or stay in BME and work on related things such as brain-computer interfaces?

I don't have an answer yet. I'll walk the road and watch as I go. At least for now, I have the courage to set out again.
